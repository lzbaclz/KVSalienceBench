\begin{abstract}
Deciding which cached attention keys and values (KV) to retain requires evaluating
predictions under the budget and candidate set of each retention decision.
KVSalienceBench audits these evaluation objects through future-attention labels,
explicit selector semantics, source-prompt splits and versioned code, and scores
pooled and per-decision metrics together.
On corrected $\le$4K-token traces from two instruction-tuned models, a
three-parameter logistic scorer reaches pooled area under the receiver operating
characteristic curve (AUC) 0.890 against LightGBM's 0.896, a gap of at most 0.008 across tree capacities and
training sizes. Yet the within-layer attention signal recovers 0.659 of each
decision's future top-decile blocks, versus 0.613 and 0.615 for those models.
Pooled AUC is almost entirely cross-decision pairs: the logistic scorer's binary
cross-layer term raises it by 0.017 but lowers within-decision AUC and recall, and
the reversal narrows at 20\% retention. A pinned bfloat16 masked-loop rerun finds
a runtime-reconstructed scorer and an H2O-style accumulator equivalent in mean LongBench
F1 within a practical margin of two absolute points over seven datasets, both
below full cache. A feature bridge leaves a historical served-oracle gap
unexplained, and its on-policy arm ranks only surviving blocks. A physical
reference backend stores only the retained KV, but extended mask-versus-compaction
replay exceeds its numerical tolerance, so no serving equivalence is claimed. These
bounded findings support evaluating prediction at the selector's operating point,
not a serving or per-task equivalence claim.
\end{abstract}
