\begin{abstract}
A scorer that predicts which cached attention keys and values (KV) to retain can be
ranked by a metric pooled over blocks from many retention decisions, whereas a
selector acts on one decision's candidate set and budget. KVSalienceBench records
candidate sets, future-attention labels, source-prompt identities and versioned
feature realizations, and scores pooled ranking together with selection under
per-decision budgets. On traces of two instruction-tuned models collected with a
4K-token input cap, a three-parameter logistic scorer and LightGBM reach pooled area
under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) 0.890 and 0.896. Yet a
within-layer attention signal recovers 0.659 of each decision's future top-decile
blocks, against 0.613 and 0.615 for those models. An exact pair decomposition and a
scaling of the scorer's cross-layer coefficient show that better cross-decision
ordering coexists with worse within-decision ranking and top-decile recall; the
reversal holds on a source-disjoint split and narrows at 20\% retention. A separate
pinned masked-loop evaluation finds two complete runtime policies equivalent in mean
LongBench F1 within a practical margin of two absolute points over seven datasets,
both below full cache. A physical reference backend stores only the retained KV but
does not establish numerical equivalence with masking. These results support
evaluating a retention scorer at its own candidate set and budget, then checking task
quality on the policy's own trajectory; they make no serving or per-task equivalence
claim.
\end{abstract}
