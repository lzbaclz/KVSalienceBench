\section{Limitations and artifact audit}
\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{External validity.} Historical traces cover four instruction-tuned 7--8B
families; corrected v2 traces and the pinned rerun cover two, at up to about 4K
context. The archived 16K Llama and short-context Qwen2.5-14B runs are separate
checks, not v2 replications (14B F1 0.301/0.307/0.301 for the accumulator,
reconstructed scorer and adaptive allocation). Nothing is extrapolated to MoE, base
checkpoints, 32K contexts or sampling; decoding is greedy.

\textbf{Query-coordinate audit.} The legacy extractor hooks \texttt{q\_proj} before
rotary transformation but reads post-rotation keys, so it never computed the
attention-space query--key product (its revision is unpinned); the old dot-max
probe is no faithful Quest control and we claim no intrinsic query redundancy.
Version 2 aligns queries and keys after RoPE, with a one-head test that its
scaled dot products reproduce the attention probabilities; even so a dot-max
probe is not Quest's page-bound algorithm. Adding it to the two magnitude views changes
held-out \emph{request-mean} AUC by $-0.0070$ on the 128 Llama prompts (32 test
requests, all negative; $[-0.0082,-0.0058]$; two-view mean 0.899) and by $+0.0020$ on
the matched Qwen prompts (31/32 positive; $[0.0016,0.0024]$; mean 0.884): small,
model-dependent, opposite in sign.

\textbf{Generation, labels and the archived loop.} The legacy collector refed the final
prompt token and clamped unavailable horizons to the last recorded step; version 2
consumes the prefill-predicted token and generates genuine lookahead, so archived
long-horizon results are not evidence for long-horizon generalization (no archived
number was silently changed). The archived cells came from an unpinned float16
simulator whose
Qwen2.5 path emits all-NaN logit rows (\S\ref{sec:transfer-gap}), scored against one
reference with articles removed before punctuation; the rerun pins the revision by hash,
uses bfloat16 for both architectures and applies one memory patch (each layer's
already-ingested attention weights replaced by a placeholder in the tuple returned to the
decoder layer), verified on a tiny model to change no statistic, mask or token. No rerun
latency is reported; the GPUs were shared.

\textbf{Tied scores and shared prompts.} The archived AUPRC integrated one row at a time
after a stable sort, crediting tied scores for row order; the two discrete probes cannot
be recomputed, so Table~\ref{tab:v2} uses the threshold-based definition. The archived
split held out model-requests, not source texts; version 2 adds a source-prompt split.

\textbf{Inference, aggregation and targets.} Information estimates depend on binning and
conditional sparsity; overlapping marginal intervals are not an equivalence test; the
primary TOST treats the seven dataset means as independent units, so it holds for this
fixed collection of two architectures and seven tasks, exploratory samples do not
replicate it, and non-rejection is not proof of no benefit. The collector averages
attention with equal weight over all query heads and over a block's actual members,
whereas the loop averages head groups and pads ragged tail blocks
(Table~\ref{tab:protocol}), a difference we did not measure. The label is the attention
four steps ahead while the loop re-decides every eight steps; a window target is
untested. Both learned scorers minimize a pooled loss (a per-decision ranking objective
is untested); a budget shared across layers or requests makes cross-decision ordering
the operating point; and offline candidates include sink and recent blocks the loop
always keeps.

\textbf{Parity levels of the reference backend.} The committed gates (1024 tokens, four
decode steps) pass their numeric tolerance. Extending them to four prompts from both
datasets, 1024--4096 input tokens and 40 decode steps with repeated eviction separates
three levels: the session reproduces native full-cache logits exactly (maximum error 0
in all 12 runs); but the compact and masked paths, fed the same teacher tokens and
schedules, drift apart over successive evictions to a maximum logit difference of
0.66--1.44 in bfloat16 (3.5--5.9$\times$ the preset tolerance) and
$3.6\times10^{-5}$--$1.9\times10^{-4}$ in float32 (1.3--6.1$\times$), so the extended
test fails that tolerance. The four-order fall with precision fits floating-point
ordering over 40 evictions but does not exclude a semantic difference; settling it needs
a layer-by-layer comparison of retained indices, positions and K/V values against a
higher-precision reference. Top-1 agreement is 972/984 gated steps in bfloat16 and 984/984 in float32.
Tolerances were not raised. The old physical-memory probe, which swallowed decode
exceptions and put full prefill in its peak statistic, is retired, its source and
results kept.

\textbf{Availability.} The artifact contains LaTeX, result JSON, analysis code,
predictor weights, tests, the version-2 collector, the reference validator and the
rerun's exact simulator source, bundled with its MIT notice and verified against all 70
cells' provenance hashes, plus a CPU example; model weights
remain separately licensed. Code, records and figure sources are at
\url{https://github.com/lzbaclz/KVSalienceBench}; the default benchmark entry scores a
prediction table without traces or weights, and raw traces are available on request.
