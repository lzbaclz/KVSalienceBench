\section{Historical diagnostics}
\label{sec:historical}
Every measurement here comes from the unpinned version-1 pipeline, whose
defects---float16 Qwen2.5 NaN logits, a query extractor that never formed the
attention-space product, an AUPRC that credited tied scores for row order---are in
\S\ref{sec:limits}. Its raw rows are gone, so none of these numbers is version-2
evidence or can be recomputed. They motivated the audit, and
\texttt{docs/HISTORICAL\_DIAGNOSTICS.md} lists each with its source. On 43,931,784
rows from four instruction-tuned families the two-view fit trailed LightGBM by 0.0010
AUC (0.948 against 0.949), the ordering the corrected corpus shows at a wider margin;
the workload-extension, transfer, online-update, risk-threshold and scoring-cost
measurements (Exp\#2--5) and the conditional-information grids are archived there, and
no version-2 claim rests on them.

\textbf{Exp\#7: Offline and in-loop targets differ.} The archived offline two-view
AUC is 0.948, while on the served-oracle calibration set the reconstructed scorer
reaches 0.643 ($[0.632,0.655]$, per-layer 0.54--0.76). We report the drop but do not
rely on it: the bridge of \S\ref{sec:realization} reproduces neither its size nor its
direction on version-2 traces, and target, trajectory and feature realization change
together here, so its cause is unidentified. The served oracle of a decode step is,
per layer, the $K=\min\{n,\max(32,\lfloor0.1n\rfloor)\}$ blocks with the largest
unmasked attention at that step; a policy's miss is the share of them outside its kept
set of $B=\max(1,\mathrm{round}(0.2n))$ blocks, mandatory blocks included. On four
LongBench tasks at 20\% retention
(48 requests each) the H2O-style accumulator has request-mean served-oracle miss 0.579
against 0.623 for the reconstructed scorer (paired difference $[0.033,0.055]$) while
the per-task F1 intervals all overlap; the confirmatory comparison is Exp\#8. A budget
below the oracle set forces a miss of at least $F=\max(0,1-B/K)$ at that step. From the
logged layer-mean counts, averaged over steps and then requests as the miss is, the
4K-cap floor is 0.219 ($K=32$ at every step, $B\le26$), leaving residuals 0.360 and
0.404. The floor is identical for the two policies on every request, so subtracting
it leaves the paired contrast unchanged algebraically: it shows how much of the
absolute miss no selector could avoid and is not an independent robustness check. A
feedback-shift check and the other caps and budget fractions are in the artifact.
