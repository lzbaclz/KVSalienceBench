\section{Problem and evidence levels}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Attention-defined saliency}
At layer $\ell$ and decode step $t$, let $\mathcal B_{\ell,t}$ contain the logical
KV blocks currently under consideration. A block groups contiguous original token
positions. We aggregate attention by averaging over heads and over a block's token
positions; denote the resulting statistic by $a_{\ell,b,t}$. It is a block-mean
attention statistic, not necessarily a mass normalized to sum to one over blocks.
For a horizon $h$ and a fraction $r$, the target is
$z_{\ell,b,t}^{(h)}=\mathbf 1[b\in\operatorname{Top}_{\lceil r|\mathcal B_{\ell,t}|\rceil}\{a_{\ell,b',t+h}:b'\in\mathcal B_{\ell,t}\}]$:
ranking is restricted to blocks present at $t$. We use $r=0.1$ and report $h=4$;
horizons 1, 16 and 64 are recorded only.

A scorer $f(\mathbf x_{\ell,b,t})$ estimates the label probability from recorded
features; examples arrive in decode order, positives are a small fraction of blocks,
drift is a hypothesis to test, and serial dependence means millions of block rows are
not millions of independent observations.

\subsection{Three different evaluation targets}
Fig.~\ref{fig:paths} locates the evaluation paths. Offline prediction scores
future-attention labels on fixed full-cache traces; served-oracle diagnostics score
a current-attention target along the evaluated trajectory, and attention importance
is not answer correctness; closed-loop task quality scores answers the selector's
own decisions helped produce, here through masking rather than a cache manager. A
fraction of blocks allowed to attend is a \emph{logical retention budget}, not of
device memory.

\subsection{Statistical protocol}
Offline ranking uses AUC, threshold-based AUPRC, precision/recall at $k=r$, Brier
score and, for scorers that emit probabilities only, expected calibration error
(ECE). Pooled
metrics rank rows \emph{across} decisions; top-$k$ metrics are reported pooled over
test rows \emph{and} per cache decision (request, layer, step) at the label's own
$\lceil r\,n\rceil$, and pooled AUC is split exactly into same-decision and
cross-decision pairs. Training and testing use disjoint requests and paired
bootstrap intervals resample requests, not block rows. Unless marked otherwise,
intervals are 95\%; equivalence intervals are 90\%. A 10,000-draw sensitivity analysis jointly resamples held-out model-requests that
share a source prompt, on both splits.
Top-$k$ score ties use a stable descending sort, and v2 label boundaries keep the
collector's \texttt{argpartition} rule with exactly $\lceil rn\rceil$ positives;
neither is randomized, the artifact states both orders exactly, and recomputing
every per-decision recall under exact random tie-breaking moves none by more than
$2\times10^{-5}$.
The main downstream comparison aggregates paired request scores into
architecture--dataset cells and averages the two architectures within each dataset;
a TOST analysis~\cite{schuirmann1987tost,lakens2017equivalence} asks whether the
mean difference over the seven datasets lies inside a practical margin. Failing to
detect a difference is not equivalence, and two comparisons equivalent to one
baseline need not be equivalent to each other.
