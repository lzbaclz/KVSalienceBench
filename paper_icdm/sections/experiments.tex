\section{Prediction measurements}
\label{sec:exp}

\textbf{Data and comparators.} Corrected version-2 traces carry the main offline
evidence; historical extensions are labeled separately. Single-signal rows score
one recorded feature and are not
reimplementations of the papers that motivate them; learned comparators are LightGBM
with 150 trees (a tree count, not a parameter count), a fitted MLP, a one-pass
online logistic baseline and the compact family.

\subsection{Exp\#1: Ranking and calibration}
\label{sec:exp-relevance}
\textbf{Version-2 corpus.} Table~\ref{tab:v2} uses checksum-verified traces:
phase-aligned queries, the prefill-predicted first token,
genuine 64-step lookahead labels, 32 feature steps, and the same 128 LongBench QA
prompts (64 Qasper, 64 NarrativeQA) for Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B, giving
30.4M rows and 256 model-requests. We sample 120K training rows and 150K held-out
rows for pooled metrics; per-decision metrics use every held-out row, and AUPRC
is threshold-based. A source-prompt-level split, holding out the same 32
prompts for both models, agrees with the request-level AUCs to three decimals for
the two-view fit and LightGBM and within 0.006 for every method; this split control
shows no substantial change, not proof of independence. The two-view fit reaches
AUC 0.890 (source-prompt bootstrap $[0.882,0.898]$) against 0.896
for LightGBM, a paired deficit of $-0.0057$ $[-0.0068,-0.0046]$, larger than the
archived $-0.0010$ but still small; the archived two-view weights, applied frozen
to the version-2 features, achieve the same rounded AUC (0.890), with different
top-$k$ values (Table~\ref{tab:v2}), and are slightly
less calibrated (ECE 0.019 versus 0.006). The four-view fit is worse (0.873):
dropping its cosine query proxy \emph{raises} AUC by 0.012, and the phase-aligned
dot-max probe adds 0.001 to the two views (0.891), a pooled near-zero that averages
over opposite signs in the tested configurations (\S\ref{sec:limits}). At the per-decision operating point
the ordering changes: the raw within-layer EMA recovers 0.659 of each decision's
future top-decile blocks against 0.613 for the two-view fit and 0.615 for LightGBM,
though its pooled top-decile precision (0.542) and AUC (0.873) are lower. Paired
over the 64 held-out requests the gaps are $+0.046$ $[0.043,0.049]$ and $+0.044$
$[0.041,0.047]$, positive on every request. These intervals use 10,000 joint
draws of 57 source prompts. On the source-disjoint split (32 prompts, both models
held out), the respective gaps are $+0.047$ $[0.044,0.050]$ and
$+0.045$ $[0.043,0.048]$. The reversal belongs to the operating
point, not the scorers: at the 20\% the closed loop budgets, per-decision recall is
0.840, 0.838 and 0.830 and the gaps fall to $+0.002$ $[0.002,0.003]$ (52/64
requests) and $+0.010$ $[0.009,0.011]$. A pooled ranking spends the budget unevenly
across decisions and rewards cross-decision calibration a per-step budget never
uses; the cost of that depends on the budget, which is why we report the operating
point a policy imposes. The smaller gap at 20\% is consistent with, but does not
explain, Exp\#8's downstream mean-F1 equivalence. Relevance
and reliability are in Fig.~\ref{fig:redundcalib}: the magnitude views carry
$I(X_i;Z)$ of 0.104 and 0.099, the query proxies 0.007 (cosine) and 0.025
(dot-max), the age proxy under 0.001; the class-balanced LightGBM has ECE 0.208
while the unweighted (0.005) and isotonic (0.003) variants match the two-view fit
(0.006) on identical test rows. For the tree calibration controls, 48 of the
192 training model-requests are reserved for isotonic fitting; the base trees
use the other 144. The two-view fit uses all 192 training requests; the 64 test
requests are excluded from both fitting stages. This is request-level calibration
separation, not source-level independence within the training set.

\textbf{Historical context.} The unpinned v1 corpus contains 43,931,784 rows
from four instruction-tuned families, 32 chat prompts each, with positive rate
0.105; 32 requests are held out. Its two-view AUC is 0.948 $[0.942,0.954]$
against LightGBM's 0.949 (paired deficit $-0.0010$ $[-0.0016,-0.0003]$).
The full historical tables, calibration and repair details remain in the artifact;
they are not additional v2 evidence.

\subsection{Exp\#2: Workload extensions and transfer}
\label{sec:exp-extval}
The archived workload extension (chat, multi-/single-document QA, code completion,
summarization; English and Chinese; 128 prompts per workload from each of the four
families) keeps both magnitude views useful (single-view AUC about 0.88--0.90 and
0.81--0.86), with pooled AUC 0.929 against 0.934 for LightGBM (AUPRC 0.777 versus
0.803 under the archived row-order tie rule; the original rows are unavailable,
so tie-aware AP cannot be recomputed) and a gap of about 0.016 AUC on short multi-turn chat. A shared
standardized four-weight predictor has mean cross-model AUC 0.920 against 0.921
within model (drop 0.0012), an offline transfer measurement only.

\subsection{Exp\#3: Temporal variation and threshold behavior}
\label{sec:exp-drift}\label{sec:exp-coverage}
In the archived within-generation split (fit on steps 0--77, test on 102--127) a
frozen scorer keeps AUC 0.948, about the same as an oracle refit, whereas the online
IRLS update reaches 0.919 (evidence against that update, not against online
learning); across forced topic switches, salient-set Jaccard falls to about 0.35
from 0.83 within a turn. At target miss 0.1 a fixed threshold of 0.5 gives
second-half miss 0.274 at retention 0.095, an offline-calibrated split-conformal
threshold 0.087 at retention 0.264 and the adaptive update 0.100 at the same
retention: the calibrated static threshold carries the result, and no long-horizon
trend is claimed from the old collector.
