\section{Conclusion}
KV-saliency prediction and KV retention are related but distinct evaluation
problems. On version-2 traces, the ranking advantage of learned scorers in pooled
metrics reverses under per-decision top-decile budgets, less sharply at 20\%
retention. Pooled AUC is almost entirely cross-decision pairs; the logistic
scorer's binary cross-layer term raises it and lowers within-decision AUC and
recall, promoting previous-layer-hot blocks that are positive less often than the
blocks they displace. A separate pinned bfloat16 masked-loop rerun finds the
reconstructed scorer mean-F1 equivalent to the H2O-style accumulator within a
practical $\pm0.02$ margin over seven datasets, as two complete runtime policies,
while both lose quality relative to full cache. The bridge puts reference-backend
feature reconstruction within 0.003 AUC; its on-policy arm ranks only surviving
blocks, so the archived
served-oracle gap stays a historical anomaly for want of a label-only arm. The physical
reference shows what storage a compact cache needs, not that it is equivalent: its
extended replay exceeds the numerical tolerance. These findings concern the tested data and implementations, not the
impossibility of better query-aware selection. To evaluate a retention scorer, fix
the candidate set and budget, measure inside each decision, then check answer quality
on the policy's own trajectory; the artifact's default protocol does the first two.
